**Verdict:** the route is sound only for the full Aoyagi-style flag/pivot atlas, with every pivot chart and the Schur strict-transform theorem included. Then it is a genuine resolution route, not just an upper-bound chart family. If one keeps only selected pivots, or replaces the Schur step by an unproved “clean factor” identity, the proof has a real gap.

**(a1) Local Cover**

Yes. A per-node blow-up of a coordinate center `I=(z_1,...,z_c)` is covered by the standard affine charts
`z_i=x`, `z_j=x v_j` for `j≠i`. The “pivot = 1” is the homogeneous direction coordinate, not a claim that the original coordinate is nonzero at the exceptional point. With all pivots included, the blow-up covers both the punctured complement and the exceptional fibre.

The composite tree is exhaustive if the centers are the strict transforms of the admissible nested rank strata. Every point of `{C_L...C_1=0}` has an admissible partial-rank vector with final rank `0`; all such strata are reached by choosing nonzero minors/pivots where the rank is positive, and rank-boundary cases fall into lower-rank branches. Non-admissible rank vectors, e.g. product rank nonzero, are not in the zero fibre and must not be blown up as centers.

**(a2) No Undershoot**

The dangerous failure mode is real in principle: if the algorithm introduced a divisor from a non-admissible or non-regular center, it could give a fake ratio below the true RLCT. The safeguard is not codimension alone; it is multiplicity control.

For the genuine recursion, each new exceptional divisor has `k_E=1`: after a coordinate-center blow-up, each center generator picks up one factor `x`, so the squared loss picks up exactly `x^2`. The Jacobian contributes `x^{c-1}`, where `c` is the coordinate-center codimension, giving ratio `c/2`.

The Schur step must be used in its squeeze form:

`c1 * Φ <= F <= c2 * Φ`, with `Φ = Σ regular squares + ||reduced chain||^2`.

It is not generally a literal Frobenius-norm equality after unimodular changes, since those changes are not orthogonal. The load-bearing algebra is the Schur ideal-membership/zero-set statement: `F-Φ` lies in the ideal generated by the regular coordinates. That prevents hidden higher-order vanishing from entering at the regular block. The reduced core may still be singular, but it is again a smaller matrix-chain core, so its divisors are handled recursively rather than treated as a single high-multiplicity divisor.

**(b) Termination**

Yes, provided every nonterminal Schur step produces a genuinely smaller reduced chain. The sound measure is

`ΣM = Σ_s M_s`.

At a nonterminal pivot, at least one positive-dimensional piece is removed, so `ΣM' < ΣM`. Thus infinite descent is impossible. A stuck node would require: still singular, but no active coordinate/pivot and no smooth terminal form. For a nonzero matrix-chain core with positive relevant widths, that cannot happen: either there is a pivotable rank-defect center, or the remaining block is already a smooth sum of squares/empty residual.

The base is: `L=1` gives `||C_1||_F^2`, a pure quadratic block with RLCT `(# entries)/2`. Degenerate zero-width/empty residual cases should be treated as terminal/spectator cases, not as nodes to pivot.

**Overall**

There is no structural obstruction to the full route. The proof hinges on these facts:

1. full affine pivot cover at every blow-up;
2. centers are smooth coordinate subspaces indexed only by admissible zero-product rank strata;
3. Schur strict transform/squeeze re-identifies the residual as a smaller matrix-chain core;
4. every new exceptional divisor has multiplicity `k=1`;
5. `ΣM` strictly drops until the smooth base case.

Without facts 2-3, the tree is only a candidate and can either miss branches or introduce spurious low-ratio divisors. With them, the atlas min equals the genuine local RLCT.
