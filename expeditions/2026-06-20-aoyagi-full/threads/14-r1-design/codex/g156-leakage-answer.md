Short answer: the proposed `R − ∏S_s ∈ ideal(E)` statement is false in general. The exact algebra gives an internal near-identity insertion between reduced factors, and matrix-product zero divisors prevent unconditional two-sided comparability.

**Exact Algebra**
Write each layer as
\[
C_s=\begin{pmatrix}A_s&Y_s\\ Z_s&T_s\end{pmatrix},
\qquad A_s=I+X_s,
\]
with \(A_s\) invertible, and set
\[
K_s:=Z_sA_s^{-1},\qquad S_s:=T_s-Z_sA_s^{-1}Y_s=T_s-K_sY_s.
\]
Then
\[
C_s=
\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\ K_s&I\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}A_s&Y_s\\0&S_s\end{pmatrix}.
\]

For \(L=2\), define
\[
H:=A_2+Y_2K_1.
\]
Then
\[
P_{00}=HA_1,\qquad
P_{01}=HY_1+Y_2S_1,
\]
\[
P_{10}=(K_2H+S_2K_1)A_1,
\]
and a direct Schur-complement calculation gives
\[
R=P_{11}-P_{10}P_{00}^{-1}P_{01}
=S_2\bigl(I-K_1H^{-1}Y_2\bigr)S_1.
\]
Hence
\[
R-S_2S_1=-S_2K_1H^{-1}Y_2S_1.
\]
This is the constructive cofactor formula. The coefficient \(H^{-1}\) is bounded near \(w^\ast\), and the correction tends to zero, but it is not generated by the final residual blocks \(E=(P_{00}-I,P_{01},P_{10})\).

For general \(L\), let
\[
P^{[j]}:=C_j\cdots C_1,
\qquad
K^{[j]}:=P^{[j]}_{10}(P^{[j]}_{00})^{-1}.
\]
For \(j\ge2\), set
\[
H_j:=A_j+Y_jK^{[j-1]}
=P^{[j]}_{00}(P^{[j-1]}_{00})^{-1},
\]
\[
G_j:=I-K^{[j-1]}H_j^{-1}Y_j.
\]
Then, assuming all partial pivots are invertible near \(w^\ast\),
\[
R=S_LG_LS_{L-1}G_{L-1}\cdots S_2G_2S_1.
\]
Thus, with \(\Delta_j:=G_j-I=-K^{[j-1]}H_j^{-1}Y_j\),
\[
R-\prod_{s=1}^L S_s
=
\sum_{j=2}^L
S_LG_L\cdots S_{j+1}G_{j+1}S_j
\Delta_j
S_{j-1}\cdots S_1.
\]
Each summand is explicitly in the ideal generated by entries of the partial lower leakage \(P^{[j-1]}_{10}\) and the next upper gauge block \(Y_j\), with bounded cofactors. It is not, in general, in the ideal generated by the final \(E\).

**Counterexample To `ideal(E)`**
Take \(L=2\), \(r=1\), reduced dimension \(2\), \(A_1=A_2=1\). Let
\[
K_1=\varepsilon e_2,\quad Y_2=\varepsilon e_1^T,\quad
S_1=\varepsilon E_{11},\quad S_2=\varepsilon E_{12}.
\]
Choose
\[
Y_1=-Y_2S_1,\qquad K_2=-S_2K_1.
\]
Then \(P_{00}=1\), \(P_{01}=0\), \(P_{10}=0\), so \(E=0\). But
\[
\prod S_s=S_2S_1=0,
\]
while
\[
R=S_2(I-K_1Y_2)S_1=-\varepsilon^4E_{11}\ne0.
\]
So \(R-\prod S_s\) cannot lie in `ideal(E)`, and the inequality
\[
\|R\|^2\le \gamma\|\prod S_s\|^2
\]
fails in every neighborhood. A similar zero-divisor choice gives the reverse failure.

**Clean Cert Statement**
Unconditionally, neither direct comparability nor “modulo \(\|E\|^2\)” comparability is true with the same final \(E\). The cert conjuncts
\[
\|R\|^2\le \gamma_2\,\|\prod S_s\|^2,
\qquad
\|\prod S_s\|^2\le \gamma_1\,\|R\|^2
\]
need an extra non-cancellation hypothesis.

A usable sufficient hypothesis is:

\[
\tag{NC}
\prod_s\|S_s(w)\|\le \kappa\|\prod_s S_s(w)\|
\quad\text{near }w^\ast.
\]

Since \(G_j\to I\), let \(\delta=\sup_j\|G_j-I\|\). The telescoping formula gives
\[
\|R-\prod S_s\|
\le C_L\delta\prod_s\|S_s\|.
\]
Under `(NC)`, shrink the neighborhood so \(C_L\delta\kappa<\alpha<1\). Then
\[
(1-\alpha)\|\prod S_s\|\le \|R\|\le(1+\alpha)\|\prod S_s\|,
\]
hence the cert constants may be taken as
\[
\gamma_2=(1+\alpha)^2,\qquad
\gamma_1=(1-\alpha)^{-2}.
\]

This `(NC)` holds automatically in the scalar reduced-core case, i.e. all intermediate reduced dimensions are \(1\). It is not implied by mere germ-nonvanishing of \(\prod S_s\); a fact like `dlnLoss_deepest_core_ne_zero` is too weak unless it encodes this stronger no-zero-divisor/no-cancellation property.
