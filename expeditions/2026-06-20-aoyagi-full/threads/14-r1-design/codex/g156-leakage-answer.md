**Notation.** Write one framed layer as
\[
M_s=\begin{pmatrix}A_s&Y_s\\ Z_s&T_s\end{pmatrix},\qquad A_s=I+X_s,
\]
and assume \(A_s\) is invertible. Put
\[
u_s=A_s^{-1}Y_s,\qquad \ell_s=Z_sA_s^{-1},\qquad
S_s=T_s-Z_sA_s^{-1}Y_s .
\]
Then
\[
M_s=
\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\ \ell_s&I\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}A_s&0\\0&S_s\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}I&u_s\\0&I\end{pmatrix}.
\]

**L=2 Formula**
For \(M_2M_1\), set
\[
K_1=I+u_2\ell_1,\qquad
H_1=I-\ell_1K_1^{-1}u_2=(I+\ell_1u_2)^{-1}.
\]
Then the Schur complement of the full product is
\[
R_2=S_2H_1S_1.
\]
Hence, for the per-layer reduced product \(Q_2=S_2S_1\),
\[
R_2-Q_2
=
S_2(H_1-I)S_1
=
-S_2\ell_1(I+u_2\ell_1)^{-1}u_2S_1.
\]
Substituting back:
\[
R_2-Q_2
=
-S_2Z_1A_1^{-1}
\bigl(I+A_2^{-1}Y_2Z_1A_1^{-1}\bigr)^{-1}
A_2^{-1}Y_2S_1.
\]
So the exact leakage is generated by the inter-layer pair \((Z_1,Y_2)\), with bounded analytic/rational coefficients near \(w^\*\).

**General L Formula**
Let \(M^{(k)}=M_k\cdots M_1\), and factor
\[
M^{(k)}
=
\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\ \lambda_k&I\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_k&0\\0&R_k\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}I&\nu_k\\0&I\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Then \(R_k\) is the full Schur complement of \(M^{(k)}\), with
\[
\lambda_1=\ell_1,\qquad R_1=S_1.
\]
Inductively, with
\[
K_k=I+u_{k+1}\lambda_k,\qquad
H_k=I-\lambda_kK_k^{-1}u_{k+1}=(I+\lambda_ku_{k+1})^{-1},
\]
one has
\[
R_{k+1}=S_{k+1}H_kR_k,
\]
and
\[
\lambda_{k+1}
=
\ell_{k+1}
+
S_{k+1}\lambda_kK_k^{-1}A_{k+1}^{-1}.
\]
Therefore
\[
R=R_L=S_LH_{L-1}S_{L-1}\cdots H_1S_1.
\]
If
\[
Q=S_LS_{L-1}\cdots S_1,
\]
then
\[
R-Q
=
\sum_{j=1}^{L-1}
S_LH_{L-1}\cdots S_{j+1}(H_j-I)S_j\cdots S_1,
\]
with
\[
H_j-I=-\lambda_j(I+u_{j+1}\lambda_j)^{-1}u_{j+1}.
\]
Constructively, every summand is in the ideal generated by entries of
\[
\lambda_j u_{j+1}.
\]
Since \(\lambda_j\) is recursively in the ideal generated by \(\ell_1,\dots,\ell_j\), the leakage lies in the inter-layer regular ideal generated by products of earlier \(Z\)-blocks with the next \(Y\)-block.

**Important Correction**
This is not, in general, the ideal generated by the full product residual
\[
E=(P_{00}-I,\;P_{01},\;P_{10}).
\]
That stronger claim is false.

Counterexample: take \(L=2\), \(r=1\), reduced size \(m=2\). Let
\[
\ell_1=\eta e_1,\quad u_2=\eta e_2^T,\quad S_1=\varepsilon E_{22},\quad S_2=\varepsilon E_{11}.
\]
Then \(u_2\ell_1=0\), so
\[
H_1=I-\eta^2E_{12}.
\]
Thus
\[
Q=S_2S_1=0,
\qquad
R=S_2H_1S_1=-\varepsilon^2\eta^2E_{12}\neq 0.
\]
Choosing the outer shears \(u_1=-u_2S_1\) and \(\ell_2=-S_2\ell_1\) makes the full product satisfy
\[
P_{00}=I,\qquad P_{01}=0,\qquad P_{10}=0,
\]
so \(E=0\), while \(R-Q\neq 0\). Hence \(R-Q\notin \langle E\rangle\), and even
\[
\|R\|^2\le C(\|E\|^2+\|Q\|^2)
\]
fails in general.

**True Cert Statement**
The unconditional true statement is the modular one with the inter-layer leakage ideal \(J\), not with \(\langle E\rangle\):
\[
R\equiv Q \pmod J,
\]
where \(J\) is generated by the entries of \(\lambda_j u_{j+1}\), \(1\le j<L\). Quantitatively, on a small neighborhood,
\[
\|R-Q\|_F
\le
C\sum_{j=1}^{L-1}\|\lambda_j\|\,\|u_{j+1}\|
\]
with bounded coefficients, after absorbing bounded \(S\), \(H\), and inverse factors.

The direct cert conjuncts
\[
\|R\|^2\le \gamma_2\|Q\|^2,
\qquad
\|Q\|^2\le \gamma_1\|R\|^2
\]
are false for matrix-valued reduced cores without an extra hypothesis.

A sufficient hypothesis is the explicit bridge nondegeneracy:
\[
\|R-Q\|_F\le \theta\|Q\|_F
\quad\text{on }U,\qquad 0<\theta<1.
\]
Then
\[
(1-\theta)\|Q\|_F\le \|R\|_F\le (1+\theta)\|Q\|_F,
\]
so one may take
\[
\gamma_2=(1+\theta)^2,\qquad
\gamma_1=(1-\theta)^{-2}.
\]

This hypothesis is automatic in the scalar reduced case \(m=1\), because then
\[
R=\Bigl(\prod_{j=1}^{L-1}H_j\Bigr)Q
\]
with each \(H_j\to 1\). It is not automatic for \(m>1\).

**Near \(w^\*\)**
At \(w^\*\), \(u_s,\ell_s\to 0\), hence \(\lambda_j\to 0\), \(H_j\to I\), and \(S_s\to T_s\). Therefore
\[
Q(w^\*)=T_L(w^\*)\cdots T_1(w^\*),
\qquad
R(w^\*)=Q(w^\*)=P_{11}(w^\*)=0.
\]
But since both vanish, \(H_j\to I\) does not give a lower bound. The reduced product can vanish by matrix-alignment while the bridged product does not, and conversely. A mere germ-nonvanishing fact such as `dlnLoss_deepest_core_ne_zero` is insufficient; what is needed is either scalar/corank-one reduction or an explicit bridge/transversality assumption like the \(\theta\)-bound above.
