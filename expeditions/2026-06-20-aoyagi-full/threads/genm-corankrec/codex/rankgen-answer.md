1. **VERDICT — Yes, \(a\) is load-bearing.**  
   **[Standard fact]** Increasing \(a\) strengthens the determinant pole; generic full Gram rank \(b\le \rho\) alone does not control it.

2. **Abstract mechanism**

   - **[Standard fact]** Near a smooth rank-\((b-1)\) point of a generic \(b\times\rho\) matrix family, the degeneracy locus has real codimension \(c=\rho-b+1\).
   - **[Standard fact]** For transverse radius \(r\), \(\det(QQ^\top)\asymp r^2\), so the charge is \(\asymp r^{-a}\).
   - **[Standard fact]** Lebesgue measure contributes \(r^{c-1}\,dr\); an additional vanishing monomial \(r^\delta\) contributes further compensation.
   - **[Standard fact]** Thus the radial integral behaves as \(\int r^{c-1+\delta-a}\,dr\), finite exactly when \(a<c+\delta\).
   - **[Inference]** Hence \(a\), degeneracy codimension, and Jacobian exponents jointly determine integrability; \(b\) only determines part of the available codimension.

3. **Can \(b\le\rho\) alone suffice?**  
   **[Inference]** Not uniformly for unrestricted \(a\): sufficiently large \(a\) always defeats any fixed codimension/measure compensation. It suffices only in trivial or separately controlled regimes—e.g. \(a=0\), \(b=0\), the family avoids degeneracy, or an independent bound \(a<\rho-b+1+\delta\) is known. Without extra compensation, bare charge integrability requires \(a+b<\rho+1\); therefore \(a+b\le\rho-1\) is sufficient but stronger than necessary for the charge alone.
