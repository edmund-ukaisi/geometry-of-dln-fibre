Q1. **Genuinely new construction.**  
Fact: `rowMix` and `radialAttach` preserve the existing parameter space and only change shared monomial support / linear generator coordinates. They do not contain a parameter-dependent right-linear change
\[
\Gamma \mapsto \Delta=\Gamma Q_b
\]
nor the associated Jacobian
\[
d\Gamma = \det(Q_bQ_b^T)^{-p/2}\,d\Delta
\]
on full-rank charts. The missing piece is an **anisotropic corank-block Morse/integration lemma with variable right factor**, including rank stratification and principalisation/log-resolution of the Gram/minor ideal of `Qb`.

The “pure route” keeping `Γ` as a coordinate does not escape this. It avoids writing the Gram determinant explicitly, but then the terminal sum-of-squares ideal contains the entries of `Γ Qb + C Qp`; resolving its rank-drop/incidence locus has the same product-minor geometry relocated into a larger ideal.

Q2. **It forces a non-coordinate center.**  
For `Qb = B C`, with `B` a `2×3` matrix and `C` a `3×4` matrix, each `2×2` minor of `BC` is
\[
D_J=\sum_{K\in { [3]\choose 2}} \det(B_{[2],K})\det(C_{K,J})
\]
by Cauchy-Binet. Thus the ideal `I_2(BC)` is not a monomial ideal in the entries of `B,C`; it is a bilinear Plücker/Cauchy-Binet ideal in minors of the factors. ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cauchy%E2%80%93Binet_formula?utm_source=openai))

Coordinate/toric blow-ups only principalise monomial data supported on coordinate strata. But `V(I_2(BC))` has rank-drop points in the dense torus where every matrix entry is nonzero. Such points are invisible to coordinate-subspace centers, so a toric sequence cannot make
\[
\det(Q_bQ_b^T)=\sum_J D_J^2
\]
into `monomial × unit` on a finite cover. One needs to blow up a determinantal/incidence/Plücker locus.

Q3. **VERDICT: RESEARCH-GRADE.**  
The required theorem is:

**Principalisation/log-resolution, with normal crossings relative to existing exceptional divisors, of the maximal-minor ideal**
\[
I_2(A_{1,b}A_2)
\]
**of a matrix product**, equivalently the Cauchy-Binet/Plücker ideal generated by the `2×2` minors of `Qb`.

In ordinary mathematics this is covered by characteristic-zero embedded principalisation/log-resolution machinery, but that is a genuine resolution theorem, not a composition of the banked measure atoms. Hironaka/Bierstone-Milman-style results provide the ambient kind of theorem; formalising the specific product-minor principalisation in a Mathlib setting would be new substantial infrastructure. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/alg-geom/9508005?utm_source=openai))

Q4. **Cheapest exact check.**  
Check that `I_2(BC)` has zeros in the dense torus by saturating by the product of all entries:
\[
(I_2(BC) : (\prod b_{ij}\prod c_{ij})^\infty) \neq (1).
\]
If non-unit, the rank-drop locus is not contained in coordinate strata, so coordinate blow-ups cannot suffice.

Even cheaper witness:

\[
B=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1\\1&2&3\end{pmatrix},\quad
C=\begin{pmatrix}
3&4&5&5\\
-4&-6&-6&-8\\
2&3&3&4
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

All entries are nonzero, but
\[
BC=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&2&1\\1&1&2&1\end{pmatrix},
\]
so every `2×2` minor of `BC` vanishes. That single torus point rules out a purely coordinate/toric principalisation route.
