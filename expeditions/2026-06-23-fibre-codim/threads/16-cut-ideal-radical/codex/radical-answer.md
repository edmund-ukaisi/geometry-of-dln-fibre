**1. VERDICT**

FACT: `I_E` is radical for every `N ≥ 1`, every dimension vector, and every matrix `E`.

The answer depends only on `rank(E)`, not on the chosen pivot normal form: endpoint row/column changes identify all rank-`r` fibres. If `r > min_i d_i`, the fibre is empty and `I_E = (1)` over algebraically closed `k`. No genericity or smoothness assumption on `E` is needed.

**2. MECHANISM**

FACT: use angle (A), not (B). The clean proof is: reduce the fixed fibre to the rank-`r` open part of a type-A quiver rank locus, then use local triviality over the rank-`r` matrix orbit.

Let `p = mult` and let `J_r` be the ideal generated by the `(r+1)`-minors of `p(A)`. The scheme `Z_r = p^{-1}{rank ≤ r}` is a type-A quiver determinantal/rank locus; the standard theorem is that these type-A quiver rank loci are reduced, with orbit closures normal/Cohen-Macaulay/rational and compatibly split in the usual formulations. Buch-Fulton give the type-A composite-rank degeneracy setup, and Kinser-Rajchgot identify type-A orbit closures with Schubert-cell pieces, recovering normality, Cohen-Macaulayness, rational singularities, and compatible splitting. ([arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9804041?utm_source=openai))

Now `p^{-1}{rank = r}` is an open reduced subscheme of `Z_r`. The endpoint group `GL(V_N) × GL(V_0)` acts transitively on rank-`r` matrices, and on each standard pivot chart this orbit map has a regular section by Gaussian elimination. Hence
`p^{-1}(U) ≅ U × p^{-1}(E)`
scheme-theoretically. Since the left side is reduced and `O(U)` is faithfully flat over `k`, the fibre ring `F_E` is reduced.

**3. THE COMPONENTS**

FACT: components are indexed by maximal type-A rank arrays, equivalently by minimal lace diagrams, with exactly `r` laces running from vertex `0` to vertex `N`.

For a tuple `A`, set
`ρ_{ij}(A) = rank(A_j ... A_{i+1})` for `0 ≤ i < j ≤ N`.
A type-A orbit closure is given by inequalities `ρ_{ij}(A) ≤ ρ_{ij}` for all intervals. The components of `mult^{-1}(E)` are the intersections with the fibre of the maximal orbit closures among those with long rank `ρ_{0N} = r`.

For `N = 2`, this specializes to pairs `(a,b)` with
`a = rank A_1`, `b = rank A_2`,
`r ≤ a ≤ min(d_0,d_1)`,
`r ≤ b ≤ min(d_1,d_2)`,
`a + b ≤ d_1 + r`,
maximal under increasing `a` or `b`. The anchor `d=(2,2,2), r=1` gives `(a,b)=(2,1),(1,2)`.

FACT: the fibre is not equidimensional in general. Example: `N=2`, `d=(3,3,3)`, `rank(E)=1` has three components with generic rank pairs `(1,3),(2,2),(3,1)`, of dimensions `9,10,9`.

**4. FORMALISATION-FRIENDLY HANDLE**

Target this structural theorem:

`J_r := ((r+1)-minors of A_N...A_1)` is radical for generic type-A matrix products.

Then prove the fibre result by explicit linear algebra:

1. Change bases so `E = diag(I_r,0)`.
2. Localize the target rank-`r` orbit at the pivot minor.
3. Construct regular endpoint matrices `g_N(M), g_0(M)` with  
   `M = g_N(M) E g_0(M)^{-1}`.
4. Use equivariance to prove  
   `(P/J_r)_pivot ≅ O(rank-r pivot chart) ⊗_k F_E`.
5. Reducedness descends from the reduced left side along faithful flatness.

This avoids primary decomposition of `I_E`.

**5. THE FAILURE MODE / ONE CAVEAT**

FACT: under the stated hypotheses, there is no nilpotent or embedded-prime failure; `I_E` is radical.

The caveat is methodological: smoothness/CM is the wrong global proof. Fibres are often singular, reducible, and even non-equidimensional, so “generically smooth plus Cohen-Macaulay” does not apply to `F_E` in general. The anchor is reduced despite singular crossing; that behavior is typical, but equidimensionality is not.
