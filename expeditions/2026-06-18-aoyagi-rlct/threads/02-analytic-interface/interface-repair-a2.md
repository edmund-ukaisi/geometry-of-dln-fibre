# A2 analytic-interface repair

Source used: Aoyagi 2023 PDF only, especially Definition 1 and Lemma 1 on
PDF p. 5, the normal-crossing extraction formula on PDF p. 6, and the
post-Theorem-3 passage on PDF pp. 11-13.

Status: controller decision after xhigh scouts `Hypatia` and `Kepler`.

## Decision

Keep A0 as a concrete normal-crossing extraction boundary. Do not broaden it
into a general analytic-ideal invariance package.

The final cited interface should act on a finite normal-crossing certificate
for the actual sum-of-squares loss or for a generator family already transported
by proved elementary/certificate-level equivalences. It should output the RLCT
and pole order from the exponent data. It should not prove that two arbitrary
analytic generator families have the same RLCT.

## What the single citation may contain

The allowed cited theorem may include:

- finite analytic charts of a resolution/principalization;
- nonvanishing unit factors on each chart;
- loss exponents `k_j` and Jacobian/prior exponents `h_j`;
- the finite-cover formula

```text
lambda = min_chart min_{j : k_j > 0} (h_j + 1) / (2 k_j),
theta  = max over minimum charts of the number of variables attaining lambda;
```

- the convention that variables with `k_j = 0` do not contribute a finite
  ratio;
- smooth compactly supported prior/weight hypotheses, with the nonvanishing
  condition at the base point.

This is the normal-crossing-to-RLCT extraction theorem.

## What remains outside A0

These are not part of A0 unless the operator explicitly expands the citation
boundary:

- Aoyagi Lemma 1 as a general RLCT comparison for ideals with different
  generators.
- Aoyagi Theorem 4, the global/deepest-singular-point comparison.
- General local analytic coordinate invariance.
- General regular-coordinate additivity.
- General generator replacement after multiplying by variable-dependent
  regular matrices.

Some facts usually hidden under those analytic statements have elementary
pieces that should be proved before extraction. For example, Lean can prove
that two matrix-entry families generate the same algebraic ideal, or that
entries of `F3 * F2` lie in the ideal generated by entries of `F2` and `F3`.
Those algebraic statements do not themselves imply RLCT equality; they become
useful only when the final normal-crossing certificate is constructed for the
generator family actually used.

## Post-Theorem-3 consequence

Aoyagi's PDF p. 13 moves from Theorem 3 to

```text
lambda <prod A^(s) - prod A*^(s)>
  = (-r^2 + r(H^(1) + H^(L+1))) / 2
    + lambda <prod C^(s)>.
```

This line is not a consequence of block algebra alone. It uses at least:

1. transport from the original product-difference generators to the block
   generators;
2. removal of the lower-right `F3 F2` term;
3. the regular-coordinate contribution from `C1 - I`, `F2`, and `F3`;
4. preservation of pole order under the same transformation.

Under the current citation rule, do not cite this as regular-coordinate
additivity. Instead, construct a full normal-crossing certificate whose
regular-variable part is present explicitly, and prove the resulting finite
exponent arithmetic.

## Preferred route

1. Prove elementary matrix-entry ideal algebra in Lean:

```text
<entries(P * A)> = <entries(A)>              if det(P) is a unit,
<entries(A * Q)> = <entries(A)>              if det(Q) is a unit,
<X, F2, F3, D - F3 F2> = <X, F2, F3, D>.
```

2. Reproduce a regular-suspension certificate: from a reduced normal-crossing
   certificate for `<prod C^(s)>`, build a certificate for

```text
<C1 - I, F2, F3, prod C^(s)>
```

whose finite exponent formula shifts `lambda` by

```text
(r^2 + r(H^(1)-r) + r(H^(L+1)-r)) / 2
  = (-r^2 + r(H^(1)+H^(L+1))) / 2
```

and leaves the pole order unchanged.

3. Use the final normal-crossing extraction citation only once, on the full
   certificate.

## Kill conditions

- A proof step says "same ideal, therefore same RLCT" before a
  normal-crossing certificate is available.
- A regular variable is appended to exponent data without constructing the
  charts that make the additive shift true.
- A determinant-unit or local-invertibility condition is used without a chart
  hypothesis.
- A pole-order count is taken in a chart that does not realize the global
  minimum.
- A final theorem still depends on Aoyagi Theorem 4 while claiming A0 is the
  only analytic citation.
