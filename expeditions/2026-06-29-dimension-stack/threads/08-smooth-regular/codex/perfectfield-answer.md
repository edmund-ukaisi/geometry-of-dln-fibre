**(a)** Yes. For a maximal ideal of a finite-type `k`-algebra, Zariski’s lemma gives `κ(m)=A/m` finite over `k`; if `k` is perfect, every finite extension is separable. Hence `κ(m)/k` is finite separable, therefore étale/formally smooth, so the `H₁` obstruction vanishes. ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zariski%27s_lemma?utm_source=openai))

**(b)** No. The count is valid over the residue field `κ(m)`, not over `k`. Algebraic closedness is only needed for the shortcut “closed points are `k`-points” or for naive Jacobian calculations at `k`-rational points. A residue-field-relative cotangent calculation `κ(m) ⊗_R Ω[R/k]` has no hidden `κ(m)=k` assumption.

**(c)** No. The implication `dim R = finrank_κ(m/m²)` iff regular is pure Noetherian local commutative algebra: regular local means the maximal ideal can be generated by exactly `dim R` elements. No field-theoretic input enters except having a residue field over which `m/m²` is measured. ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_local_ring?utm_source=openai))

**(d)** No, `[PerfectField k]` is not weakest. It is a clean global sufficient hypothesis ensuring all closed-point residue fields are separable, but the pointwise hypothesis needed for step 2 is just that this particular `κ(m)/k` is formally smooth, equivalently finite separable here. The standard imperfect example `k` of characteristic `p`, `a ∉ k^p`, `A=k[x]`, `m=(x^p-a)` shows the real obstruction to the cotangent-injectivity proof: `A_m` is still regular, but `κ(m)/k` is inseparable and the conormal map can fail to be injective. So the obstruction is genuine for this proof, not for the theorem itself.

Verdict: `[PerfectField]` is sound and algebraic closedness is unnecessary, but it is **not** near-weakest for the theorem.
