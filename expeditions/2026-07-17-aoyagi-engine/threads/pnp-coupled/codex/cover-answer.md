## Q1

[FACT] Verdict: The particular leaf-chart cover is not logically necessary, but an a.e.-exhaustive substitute is; Facts 1–3 alone do not prove the lower bound.

[FACT] A lower RLCT bound asserts integrability over an entire neighbourhood, so estimates on an unverified subset cannot control omitted directions.

[FACT] Ideal-invariance changes generators by locally bounded analytic coefficients; it provides comparability, not control of every valuation or approach direction.

[FACT] A global inequality \(F\ge cH\) with \(\operatorname{rlct}_0(H)\ge M_{\min}/2\) would replace the cover completely.

[FACT] In coupled matrix dimensions, \(F^{-1}(0)\) contains cancellation points with every matrix entry nonzero, ruling out a nonzero original-coordinate monomial as such an \(H\).

[FACT] Therefore the cover is load-bearing for the proposed resolution proof, although a direct integral, valuation inequality, or global degeneration could replace it.

## Q2

[FACT] Verdict: For a genuine finite composition of full smooth blow-ups and analytic automorphisms, proving coverage is bounded atlas/properness bookkeeping, not another resolution theorem.

[FACT] Blow-ups are proper and surjective; complete projective normal-bundle atlases cover them, and properness plus compactness yields finitely many relatively compact chart pieces.

[FACT] A unipotent shear loses no points only where it and its inverse are uniformly defined; pivot inverses approaching singularity must be handed to another branch.

[FACT] Monomial Jacobians follow only if every transformed exceptional divisor remains locally normal-crossing; determinant-one shears alone do not establish this on a large chart.

[INFERENCE] Facts 1–4 do not certify that Aoyagi’s tree includes every projective chart or resolves every point where a purported unit vanishes.

[INFERENCE] Coupling creates a new bookkeeping escape—independent \(q/u\) rates and unbounded residual shears—but no new properness failure once both join charts and pivot-boundary branches are present.

[INFERENCE] Sharpest test: send \(q_n/u_n\to0\) through the coupled shear and exhibit a compact \(u\)-dominant leaf on which the shear inverse and Jacobian unit stay uniformly bounded.

[INFERENCE] Classification: **BOUNDED-REPRODUCE**

## Q3

[INFERENCE] Verdict: No lighter proof follows from the supplied facts; the most credible alternative is a matrix-product-specific valuation or Gröbner-degeneration argument.

[FACT] Layerwise homogeneity separates radial powers, but the angular product vanishes through kernel alignments, so scaling alone supplies candidates rather than integrability.

[FACT] Bounds using \(\sigma_{\min}\) degenerate precisely on the coupled rank strata and cannot simply be iterated sharply.

[FACT] A single Łojasiewicz exponent is generally too coarse: even \(x^2y^2\ge\operatorname{dist}((x,y),V(xy))^4\) yields only \(1/4\), below its RLCT \(1/2\).

[INFERENCE] SVD or conditional integration could work, but uniform estimates at zero singular-value collisions and moving kernels essentially reproduce the rank-profile joins.

[FACT] A decisive shortcut would be a monomial degeneration \(I_0\) with \(\operatorname{lct}_{\mathbb C}(I_0)=M_{\min}\): semicontinuity gives \(\operatorname{rlct}_{\mathbb R}(\sum I^2)\ge\frac12\operatorname{lct}_{\mathbb C}(I)\ge M_{\min}/2\).

[INFERENCE] Producing that degeneration—or proving \(A(v)\ge M_{\min}v(I)\) for every relevant valuation—is a plausible finite combinatorial program, but is not presently established here.
