## 1. The one structural idea underneath the whole expedition

Aoyagi's recursion has two faces, and every major event of this expedition — the four paper
defects, the type crux, the ψ adjudication, the realization gap — was an instance of these two
faces being conflated somewhere (by the paper, by us, or by a type):

- **The ledger**: profiles, exponents, cleared-counts, the case dispatch — a combinatorial
  object. Termination (μ-lex), coherence (divExp = Mval), admissibility (P ⊆ Adm), the
  minimizer's attainment all live here. The ledger tree is the SYMMETRIC QUOTIENT of the
  recursion: it deliberately forgets which chart you are in, because every chart of a node's
  family has the same ledger future. That blindness is a feature (it is why the exponent
  theory is finite and computable) — until you ask a geometric question of it.
- **The atlas**: charts, gauges, covers, Jacobians — the geometric object the integral reads.
  It fans out where the ledger quotients: one ledger node ↔ a d_center family of charts.

The corrected architecture that emerged — and this is the expedition's real contribution
beyond "the paper, in Lean" — is a clean two-layer factorization with two explicit joints:

- **Joint (C), exponent-agreement**: the atlas's per-piece exponents must lie in the ledger's
  terminalExponents. This is the ONLY place the geometry consults the ledger's numbers, and it
  is exactly what the threshold integral needs (the elder verified it feeds precisely the two
  hypotheses of the per-leaf finiteness lemma). The λ read-off is a LEDGER quantity; the atlas
  is a covering device certified against it.
- **Joint (D), fidelity**: the atlas is the fold of THIS tree's per-node families — the tie
  that makes ChartBridge denote "Aoyagi's resolution of t" and not "some monomialising cover
  agreeing with t's numbers". Everything else in the type is deliberately ledger-blind.

Instances of the theme, named: the paper's p.22 read-off implicitly assumed
ledger-indexable ⟹ geometrically-realized (defect #4: false — the realized set is exactly
Clearable-Adm, the stranding is a ledger-level phenomenon with a geometric echo); the frozen
ChartBridge quantified geometry by ledger indices (type-level instance #5); the placeholder
charts stubbed geometry INTO ledger records (instance #4, averted); the single-ψ expectation
projected a per-edge geometric structure onto a per-node ledger index (refuted — the gauge is
edge-data; and even the "merge edges are pure ledger" belief was half-wrong: the u-blow-up is
real geometry riding a ledger edge). The lesson generalises: **when a statement mixes a
ledger quantifier with a geometric predicate, stop and check which face it should live on.**
That is a taste-rule worth carrying to any future formalisation of a resolution-flavoured
argument, and it subsumes several of our standing-counsel entries as special cases.

## 2. Why the two remaining cruxes are the RIGHT residue

It is not an accident that what remains is (i) exhibit one steered path (tStar_realized) and
(ii) build the atlas for real (carrier + tide). These are the two theorems that CANNOT be
stated on one face alone: tStar_realized needs an ∃ over the tree (ledger) whose content is a
realized stratum (the geometric echo of clearability); the carrier is by definition the
ledger→geometry functor (edges gain their β/α data; nodes gain their families). Everything
that could live on one face is done and clean-three. The expedition converged to its
irreducible cross-face core — which is what "the sea rising over bedrock" should look like.
