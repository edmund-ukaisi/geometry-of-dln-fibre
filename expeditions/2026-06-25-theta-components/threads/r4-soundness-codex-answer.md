**LEMMA 1**

TRUE. For a finitely presented domain over an algebraically closed field, `Spec D` is nonempty, reduced, and irreducible, and algebraically closed implies perfect in every characteristic. Generic smoothness over a perfect field gives a dense smooth locus, and finite presentation gives openness of the smooth locus. In an irreducible affine scheme with generic point `(0)`, every nonempty open contains `(0)`, so smoothness somewhere implies smoothness at `(0)`. This is not vacuous: over an imperfect field, a reduced/domain finite type algebra can fail to be generically smooth, e.g. a purely inseparable finite field extension. Algebraic closedness is stronger than needed; perfectness is the real field hypothesis. This depends on Mathlib’s `dense_smoothLocus_of_perfectField` having the reduced + locally finite presentation + perfect-field hypotheses as stated.

**LEMMA 2**

TRUE. The KEY sublemma is true for a finitely generated `k`-domain `R` and nonzero `g`: `R[1/g]` is again a fg `k`-domain, has the same fraction field as `R`, and `dim R = trdeg_k Frac(R)` in all characteristics. The DVR counterexample is excluded: a nonfield DVR is local of dimension `1`, while a finite type algebra over a field is Jacobson, so a local finite type domain must be a field. Perfectness and algebraic closedness are irrelevant here. However, the phrase “both algebraic over `A/p`” is not sound as stated; `R[1/g]` is usually not algebraic or integral over `R`, and the correct reason is equality of fraction fields plus the affine-domain dimension theorem. Global no-drop + avoidance alone do not logically imply per-component no-drop outside the fg affine setting: take `A = k[[t]] × k[x]`, `f = (t,1)`. Both minimal primes are top-dimensional and avoid `f`, and `dim A[1/f] = dim A = 1`, but the `k[[t]]` component localizes to `k((t))`, dropping from dimension `1` to `0`. Within fg `k`-algebras that counterexample cannot occur precisely because the KEY sublemma forbids it.
