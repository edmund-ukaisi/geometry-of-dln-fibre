1. **Route G does not give the isotropic form.** The change `Γ ↦ Γ Q_b` makes the quadratic isotropic in the image variable, but exactly at the price of the Jacobian
`det(Q_b Q_b^T)^(-p/2)` and a `Q_b`-dependent image/domain. Without a uniform lower singular-value bound, dropping that factor is not a change of variables. So using Gram c.o.v. to obtain `frobSq Δ + W` with plain Lebesgue `dΔ dA'` is a category error, except in the trivial orthonormal-row case.

2. **Joint integration does not rescue Route G cleanly.** If `Q_b` is rank-deficient on positive-measure `A'`, the full-rank Gram map is not locally invertible there. Extending the full-rank formula gives an infinite determinant weight on a non-null set, which is not equivalent to the original bounded-`Γ` integral. A legitimate Gram-route proof would need rank stratification or a genuine joint principalisation/normal form for the relevant determinantal ideal, together with the residual quadratic terms. Fubini alone does not fix the degenerate strata.

3. **Verdict:** If the target consumable is the pure isotropic per-step integral with `Δ` measured by its own Frobenius norm and no Gram determinant, then `Γ ↦ Γ Q_b` is the wrong first step. It moves the proof into the Gram-Jacobian world, where degeneracy of `Q_b` is a real obstruction requiring extra resolution data. Route P is the route aligned with that isotropic endpoint, provided its radial/resolution step itself honestly produces the claimed monomial/unit control.
